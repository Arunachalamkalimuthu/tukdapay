<Lede>Only a charge on you could change what you pay, and paying in parts to get under one isn’t what TukdaPay is for. Here’s how to tell who a charge lands on, with a worked example.</Lede>

## First, check who a charge lands on

A charge on a UPI payment can fall on the shop or on you, and only a charge on you changes what you pay. NPCI’s September 2026 FAQs on the merchant discount rate ([PDF](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf)) cover consumers in question 15 and whether a shop can pass the fee on in question 34. [Here’s how to check what applies](/blog/upi-2000-threshold-what-to-check/).

## If a charge did land on you

Suppose a rule adds a 1% charge to UPI payments above ₹2,000 (an invented figure — [check what really applies](/blog/upi-2000-threshold-what-to-check/)). You owe ₹5,000.

| Way to pay                | Charge | You pay |
| ------------------------- | -----: | ------: |
| One payment of ₹5,000     |    ₹50 |  ₹5,050 |
| ₹1,999 + ₹1,999 + ₹1,002 |     ₹0 |  ₹5,000 |

On paper that’s ₹50 less, for two more PIN entries. It still isn’t a reason to pay in parts: whether a charge counts each payment or the whole bill depends on the rule’s own wording, and the shop has to agree to take the bill in parts. Pay in one go when you can.

## When it saves nothing

- **The charge is on the merchant, not you.** Then your bill is the same either way. If you pay in parts for another reason, ask the shop before you split. If you’re the merchant, see [what merchants can do](/use-cases/#merchants).
- **The charge is flat, not tiered.** If every payment costs ₹X, three payments cost 3X. Splitting makes it worse.
- **The threshold is per day, not per payment.** Then parts add up and you’re back where you started.
- **There is no charge.** Then there’s nothing to save, and one payment is simpler.

## What it costs

- One PIN entry per part.
- A merchant who has to match three payments to one bill. Put the invoice number in the note; TukdaPay adds *Part 1/3* and so on automatically.
- Daily UPI limits. If your bank caps how much you can send by UPI in a day, splitting doesn’t change the total. Here’s [where to see your UPI limits](/blog/cant-pay-more-than-2000-upi/).

## A rule of thumb

Pay in one go when you can. Paying in parts is for when one payment won’t go through, or when the shop asks for it that way, and either way, ask the shop first.
