<Lede>"₹2000" comes up a lot in UPI news — as a cap on a benefit, the start of a fee, or a reporting line. Before you change how you pay, check which one is actually in force for you today.</Lede>

<Callout>
This page deliberately doesn't state the current rule. Rules like these can be revised, and a page like this can fall behind. The only reliable sources are the ones below.
</Callout>

## Who actually sets UPI rules

- **NPCI** runs UPI. Its circulars set limits, fees and merchant categories. [npci.org.in](https://www.npci.org.in/) On 15 September 2026 it published FAQs titled *Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on Select UPI (P2M) Transactions* ([PDF](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf)).
- **RBI** sets the policy NPCI operates under, including anything about charges on digital payments. [rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in/)
- **Ministry of Finance / CBIC** decide tax questions such as GST. A rumour about "tax on UPI" is only real once it appears in a notification or a press release from them. [pib.gov.in](https://pib.gov.in/), and [PIB Fact Check](https://factcheck.pib.gov.in/) for rumours
- **Your bank and your UPI app** tell you what they charge *you*, if anything. Look for a "charges" or "fees" page in the app.

## Three different things "₹2000" can mean

### 1. A fee on the shop (MDR)

A merchant discount rate is a fee a shop pays its bank on the payments it receives. NPCI's September 2026 FAQs cover who it applies to, from when, how small shops are treated and whether a shop can pass it on to you (questions 5, 15, 23–26 and 34). Read the answers there rather than in a forward: they can be revised.

### 2. A charge on you, the customer

This is the version that spreads fastest on WhatsApp. NPCI's FAQs ask it directly (question 15: "Will ordinary consumers be charged for making payments via UPI?"). For any newer claim, look for it on [PIB Fact Check](https://factcheck.pib.gov.in/) or in an NPCI, RBI or PIB release before you believe or forward it.

### 3. A limit on a benefit

Some government incentive schemes for small merchants have covered only UPI payments up to ₹2,000. Whether one is running now, and what it covers, is announced on [pib.gov.in](https://pib.gov.in/).

## Can a shop add a fee to my UPI bill?

NPCI's FAQs answer this for the merchant fee (question 34). If a shop asks for extra on a UPI payment, ask which rule they mean and check it there.

## A ten-second check before you change how you pay

1. Search the exact rumour on [PIB Fact Check](https://factcheck.pib.gov.in/). It's the government's fact-check unit, and it publishes what it has checked there.
2. Open your UPI app's fees or charges page, or ask your bank. That's where a charge on *you* would be listed.
3. Ask the merchant. If a fee applies to them, they'll know — and if you're thinking of paying in parts, ask the shop before you split.
